Question 001. Lionel Robbins (1932) defined economics as the science that studies human behaviour as a relationship between ends and scarce means which have alternative uses. Which feature of this definition most clearly separates it from Alfred Marshall's "welfare" definition?

A. It limits economics to the study of material wealth.
B. It applies to any choice involving scarce means, whether or not material welfare is involved.
C. It treats economics as a purely normative science concerned with improving welfare.
D. It rejects the idea that resources have alternative uses.

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Marshall described economics as the study of mankind in the ordinary business of life, focusing on the material requisites of well-being. Robbins argued that the economic aspect of any behaviour is the problem of allocating scarce means among competing ends, material or not. A choice about how to use one's time, for example, is economic under Robbins's definition. Robbins also insisted economics should be neutral between ends, which makes Option C the opposite of his view.


Question 002. A fisherman can spend one day catching 20 kg of fish (worth N30,000) or repairing his neighbour's roof for N25,000, or resting. If he chooses to fish, what is the opportunity cost of his decision?

A. N55,000, the sum of the alternatives given up
B. N25,000, the value of the next best alternative given up
C. N30,000, the value of the fish caught
D. Zero, because fishing earns more than roofing

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative forgone, not the total of all alternatives. Of the options he gives up (roofing and resting), the best is roofing, worth N25,000. Option A adds alternatives that could not all have been done at once. Option D confuses a choice being worthwhile with having no cost: every choice involving a scarce resource has an opportunity cost.


Question 003. Which of the following is the best example of a free good in the economic sense?

A. Public primary education provided at no charge to pupils
B. Water supplied by a state water board without a meter
C. A gift of a textbook from a relative
D. Air in an open environment, available in quantities exceeding wants at zero price

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: A free good is one whose supply is so plentiful relative to wants that it has no opportunity cost; nobody must give anything up to obtain more. Free public education, gifts and unmetered water are all free to the user, but each uses scarce resources (teachers, paper, treatment plants) that have alternative uses, so they are economic goods. "Free of charge" is not the same as "free good".


Question 004. Every economic system must answer the basic questions of resource allocation. Which of the following is NOT one of these basic questions?

A. Whether resources should be scarce or abundant
B. How should goods and services be produced?
C. For whom should the goods and services be produced?
D. What goods and services should be produced, and in what quantities?

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: The basic economic problems are What, How and For Whom to produce (often extended to include questions of efficiency and growth). Scarcity is not a choice a society makes; it is the condition that creates these questions in the first place. If resources were not scarce, the three questions would not need answering.


Question 005. Which of the following statements is normative?

A. An increase in the minimum wage will reduce employment among unskilled workers.
B. Nigeria's inflation rate rose last year.
C. The government ought to reduce the tax burden on low-income households.
D. If the price of petrol rises, fewer litres will be bought, other things being equal.

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: A normative statement expresses a value judgement about what ought to be, and cannot be settled by evidence alone. Option C uses "ought" and rests on views about fairness. Options A, B and D are positive statements: they describe or predict what is, was or will be, and can in principle be tested against data, even if they turn out to be false. A positive statement does not have to be true to be positive.


Question 006. The rewards to the four factors of production are, respectively:

A. Land - interest; labour - wages; capital - rent; entrepreneurship - profit
B. Land - rent; labour - wages; capital - interest; entrepreneurship - profit
C. Land - rent; labour - profit; capital - interest; entrepreneurship - wages
D. Land - profit; labour - wages; capital - rent; entrepreneurship - interest

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: In the traditional classification, land (all natural resources) earns rent, labour (human effort) earns wages, capital (man-made aids to production) earns interest, and the entrepreneur, who organises the other factors and bears uninsurable risk, earns profit. Profit is a residual: what remains after the other factors are paid, and it may be negative (a loss).


Question 007. A country's production possibility curve (PPC) for food and machines is bowed outward (concave to the origin). This shape mainly reflects:

A. Constant opportunity cost, because all resources are equally suited to both goods
B. Increasing opportunity cost, because resources are not equally suited to producing both goods
C. Decreasing opportunity cost caused by economies of scale
D. Unemployment of resources in the economy

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: As the economy moves more resources into machines, it first moves those best suited to machine production. Further increases require resources better suited to food, so more food must be given up for each extra machine. This rising opportunity cost makes the curve bow outward. A straight-line PPC shows constant opportunity cost. Unemployment is shown by a point inside the curve, not by the curve's shape.


Question 008. An economy is producing at a point inside its production possibility curve. Which conclusion is correct?

A. The economy cannot increase output of one good without reducing the other.
B. Some resources are unemployed or used inefficiently, so output of both goods could rise.
C. The economy has experienced economic growth.
D. The economy is producing beyond its current capacity.

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Points on the PPC are productively efficient: more of one good requires less of the other. A point inside the curve means resources are idle or misallocated, so output of at least one good, and possibly both, can be increased without sacrificing the other. Growth is an outward shift of the curve. Points outside the curve are currently unattainable.


Question 009. Which of the following would shift a country's production possibility curve outward?

A. A fall in unemployment from 20% to 10%
B. A reallocation of workers from agriculture to manufacturing
C. A discovery of new oil reserves together with better extraction technology
D. A rise in the price of exported cocoa

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: The PPC shows maximum output given the economy's resources and technology. More resources or better technology shift it outward. Reducing unemployment (A) moves the economy from inside the curve towards it, without shifting it. Reallocating workers (B) is a movement along or towards the curve. A change in a world price (D) changes the value of output but does not by itself change productive capacity.


Question 010. Microeconomics differs from macroeconomics mainly because microeconomics:

A. Uses mathematics, while macroeconomics does not
B. Studies the behaviour of individual units such as households, firms and particular markets
C. Studies only firms, while macroeconomics studies only governments
D. Is positive, while macroeconomics is normative

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Microeconomics examines decision-making by individual households and firms and the determination of prices and quantities in specific markets. Macroeconomics studies economy-wide aggregates such as national income, the general price level, unemployment and growth. Both use mathematics, and both contain positive and normative statements.


Question 011. Which of the following questions belongs to macroeconomics rather than microeconomics?

A. Why has the price of garri risen in Ibadan markets?
B. How many workers should a bakery employ?
C. What causes the general price level in Nigeria to rise persistently?
D. How will a tax on cigarettes affect cigarette sales?

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: A persistent rise in the general price level is inflation, an economy-wide aggregate, so it is a macroeconomic question. Options A, B and D concern a single product market, a single firm's input decision, and the effect of a tax on one market, which are microeconomic.


Question 012. A student says: "Since economics deals with human behaviour, which cannot be predicted, economics cannot be a science." The strongest response is that:

A. Economics applies systematic methods to form and test hypotheses about average or typical behaviour, even if individual behaviour varies.
B. Economics is a science only because it uses graphs.
C. Economics is not a science, because all its statements are normative.
D. Economics can predict every individual's behaviour exactly.

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Economics is called a social science because it uses scientific method: observation, hypothesis formation, deduction and testing against evidence. Its laws describe tendencies that hold on average and ceteris paribus, not exact predictions about each person. Its ability to make testable, if imperfect, predictions is what makes it scientific. It cannot run controlled laboratory experiments as easily as physics, but it still contains a large body of positive analysis.


Question 013. Scarcity, in economics, exists:

A. Only in poor countries
B. In every society, because wants are unlimited relative to available resources
C. Only when prices are high
D. Only in planned economies, where markets are absent

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Scarcity is relative, not absolute. Even wealthy countries cannot satisfy every want, because time and resources are limited while wants keep expanding. Poverty is a low level of income; scarcity is the universal gap between wants and means. Every economic system, market or planned, must deal with it.


Question 014. A farmer can produce either 100 bags of maize or 50 bags of beans on his land. What is the opportunity cost of 1 bag of beans, assuming constant opportunity cost?

A. 0.5 bags of maize
B. 2 bags of maize
C. 50 bags of maize
D. 100 bags of maize

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Producing 50 bags of beans means giving up 100 bags of maize. Opportunity cost of 1 bag of beans = 100 / 50 = 2 bags of maize. Conversely, 1 bag of maize costs 50 / 100 = 0.5 bags of beans, which is Option A and is a common reversal error.


Question 015. Which of the following best describes "capital" as a factor of production?

A. Man-made goods used to produce other goods, such as tools, machines and buildings
B. Shares bought on the stock exchange
C. Money held in a bank account
D. Natural resources such as land and minerals

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: In economics, capital means physical, produced means of production. Money and shares are financial assets: they can be used to buy capital, but are not themselves inputs that produce output. Natural resources are classified as land.


Question 016. The entrepreneur is distinguished from other factors of production chiefly because he or she:

A. Provides physical effort in exchange for a fixed wage
B. Organises the other factors and bears the uninsurable risks of the business
C. Owns the land on which production takes place
D. Lends money to the firm at a fixed rate of interest

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: The entrepreneur combines land, labour and capital, makes the key decisions, and bears risk that cannot be insured, such as the risk that demand will fall. Frank Knight (1921) distinguished insurable risk from this uncertainty, and saw profit as the reward for bearing it. Wage earners, landlords and lenders receive contracted payments; the entrepreneur receives a residual that may be a loss.


Question 017. In a market economy, the basic questions of What, How and For Whom are answered mainly by:

A. A central planning authority
B. Tradition and custom
C. The price mechanism, through the decisions of consumers and producers
D. Random allocation

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: In a market economy, prices signal scarcity and coordinate decisions. Consumer spending determines what is produced; firms choose least-cost methods (how); and incomes from selling factor services determine who can buy the output (for whom). A planned economy uses a central authority; a traditional economy relies on custom; a mixed economy combines markets with government intervention.


Question 018. Which of the following is a major criticism of the command (centrally planned) economy?

A. Planners lack the detailed information on costs and preferences that prices provide, leading to shortages and surpluses.
B. It always produces extreme inequality of income.
C. It cannot provide any public goods.
D. It relies entirely on consumer sovereignty.

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Without market prices, planners must gather enormous amounts of information about preferences and production possibilities. Economists such as Ludwig von Mises and Friedrich Hayek argued that this information problem leads to misallocation: queues and shortages of some goods, unwanted surpluses of others. Command economies typically aimed at equality and provided public goods; consumer sovereignty is a feature of market economies.


Question 019. A mixed economy is best described as one in which:

A. Both private enterprise and government play significant roles in resource allocation
B. Only agricultural and industrial goods are produced
C. Foreign and domestic firms compete equally
D. Barter and money are both used for exchange

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: A mixed economy combines the price mechanism with government intervention, for example through public provision of education and roads, regulation, taxation and state-owned enterprises. Nigeria, like most modern economies, is a mixed economy. The other options describe the variety of goods, trade or means of payment, not the system of resource allocation.


Question 020. Economics is linked to political science mainly because:

A. Economists determine election results.
B. Political decisions have no economic consequences.
C. Political science uses the same mathematical models as economics.
D. Economic policies such as taxation and spending are made and implemented through political institutions.

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Classical economics was originally called "political economy" because economic policy is formed through the state. Budgets, trade policy and regulation are political decisions with economic effects, and economic conditions in turn shape politics. Fields such as public choice theory study this two-way relationship.


Question 021. Which of the following is the best example of using economics together with statistics?

A. Writing the history of the Nigerian civil war
B. Studying the chemical composition of crude oil
C. Debating whether wealth should be redistributed
D. Estimating, from household survey data, how much spending on food rises when income rises

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Statistics provides the tools for collecting and analysing data to test economic hypotheses; the combination of economics, mathematics and statistics is called econometrics. Estimating how food spending responds to income is an empirical economic measurement. Option C is a normative debate, and Options A and B belong mainly to history and chemistry.


Question 022. A government spends N10 billion on a new hospital. Economists would say the true cost to society is:

A. N10 billion, regardless of what else could have been done
B. Zero, if the money was borrowed
C. The value of the best alternative use of the resources used to build the hospital
D. Only the cost of the equipment, not the labour

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: The economic cost of a project is its opportunity cost: the value of what society gives up, such as roads or schools that could have been built with the same labour, materials and land. Money cost is often a useful measure, but the basic concept is the forgone alternative. Borrowing does not remove the cost; it shifts when it is paid.


Question 023. Which of the following is an example of the economic concept of "choice" arising from scarcity?

A. A star is visible at night.
B. The sun rises every morning.
C. A rainfall increases the water level of a river.
D. A student decides how to divide 24 hours between study, work and sleep.

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Choice arises when a limited resource (here, time) has competing uses. Allocating 24 hours means more of one activity requires less of another, so there is an opportunity cost. Options A, B and C are natural events that involve no human allocation decision.


Question 024. A community can produce the following combinations of rice and cloth: (100 rice, 0 cloth), (90 rice, 10 cloth), (70 rice, 20 cloth), (40 rice, 30 cloth), (0 rice, 40 cloth). What is the opportunity cost of increasing cloth from 20 to 30 units?

A. 10 units of rice
B. 20 units of rice
C. 30 units of rice
D. 40 units of rice

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Moving from (70 rice, 20 cloth) to (40 rice, 30 cloth), rice falls from 70 to 40, a loss of 30 units, to gain 10 units of cloth. The opportunity cost is 30 units of rice, or 3 units of rice per unit of cloth. The rising cost of each extra 10 units of cloth (10, 20, 30, 40 rice) shows increasing opportunity cost.


Question 025. Which statement correctly describes the relationship between positive and normative economics in policy-making?

A. Positive economics alone can tell a government which policy to choose.
B. Normative economics replaces the need for evidence.
C. Positive analysis predicts the consequences of policies, while normative judgements decide which consequences are preferable.
D. They are completely unrelated and never used together.

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Positive analysis can predict, for example, that a fuel subsidy lowers transport costs but raises the budget deficit. Whether that trade-off is worthwhile depends on value judgements about equity, priorities and acceptable debt, which are normative. Good policy uses both: evidence about consequences and explicit judgements about goals.


Question 026. Which of the following is an economic good?

A. Bottled water sold in a supermarket
B. Sea water in the middle of the Atlantic Ocean
C. Sunlight on a farm in the dry season
D. Wind blowing across an open field

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: An economic good is scarce relative to demand and uses resources that have alternative uses, so it carries an opportunity cost and usually a price. Bottled water needs treatment, bottling and transport. Sunlight, open sea water and wind are normally available in amounts greater than people want at zero price, so they are free goods.


Question 027. Labour as a factor of production refers to:

A. Only manual work done in factories
B. The number of people living in a country
C. All human physical and mental effort used in production
D. The machines operated by workers

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Labour covers all human effort, physical and mental, used to produce goods and services, from farming to teaching and surgery. Population is not the same as labour: children, retirees and people outside the labour force are not supplying labour. Machines are capital.


Question 028. A doctor who is also an excellent typist hires a secretary, even though the doctor types faster. This decision is best explained by:

A. Opportunity cost
B. Absolute advantage
C. Free goods
D. Diminishing returns

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Every hour the doctor spends typing is an hour not spent treating patients, which is far more valuable. The doctor's opportunity cost of typing is therefore high, even though the doctor types faster (has an absolute advantage). It is cheaper overall to hire a secretary, whose opportunity cost of typing is lower. This is the basis of comparative advantage and specialisation.


Question 029. "Land" as a factor of production includes:

A. All natural resources, such as minerals, forests, water and the land surface
B. Only agricultural farmland
C. Factory buildings and warehouses
D. Money used to buy property

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Economists use "land" for all gifts of nature used in production: soil, crude oil, fish in rivers, forests and space. Buildings are man-made, so they are capital. Money is a financial asset, not a factor of production. A distinctive feature of land is that its total supply is fixed in the long run.


Question 030. Which statement about scarcity and price is correct?

A. Every scarce good must have a high price.
B. Goods become scarce only when governments impose taxes.
C. A good can be scarce in the economic sense even if its price is low.
D. A good with a zero market price can never be scarce.

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: A good is economically scarce if more is wanted than is available at a zero price. Salt is cheap but scarce in this sense, because producing it uses resources. Price reflects the balance of supply and demand, not whether scarcity exists. Goods provided at zero price by government (for example public schooling) are still scarce, so Option D is wrong.


Question 031. Which of the following is a characteristic of a traditional economy?

A. Resources are allocated mainly by central planners.
B. Production and distribution follow custom, habit and inherited practice.
C. Prices determined in competitive markets guide all decisions.
D. Advanced technology changes production methods rapidly.

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: In a traditional economy, what to produce, how and for whom follow long-established customs, for example children taking up their parents' occupations and output being shared by kinship rules. Such economies tend to change slowly. Central planning describes command economies, and market prices describe market economies.


Question 032. Which pair consists of a microeconomic topic and a macroeconomic topic, in that order?

A. National unemployment; the price of yam
B. The output decision of a cement firm; the national inflation rate
C. Gross domestic product; the balance of payments
D. The wage of nurses in one hospital; the demand for bread

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: A single firm's output decision is microeconomic; the economy-wide inflation rate is macroeconomic. Option A is in the reverse order, Option C is two macroeconomic topics, and Option D is two microeconomic topics.


Question 033. The "fallacy of composition" warns that:

A. What is true for a part is necessarily true for the whole.
B. Microeconomics and macroeconomics always give the same results.
C. What is true for one individual may not be true for the economy as a whole.
D. Correlation always proves causation.

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: The fallacy of composition is assuming that what holds for one unit must hold for the whole. One farmer who raises output can earn more, but if all farmers raise output, prices may fall so much that total farm income falls. Similarly, one person saving more becomes richer, but if everyone saves more at once, total spending and income may fall (the "paradox of thrift"). This is one reason macroeconomics is studied separately.


Question 034. A university student gives up a job paying N40,000 per month to study full time. Tuition and books cost N20,000 per month. The monthly economic cost of studying is:

A. N20,000
B. N40,000
C. N0, because education is an investment
D. N60,000

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Economic cost includes explicit costs (tuition and books, N20,000) and the implicit cost of the forgone earnings (N40,000). Total = N20,000 + N40,000 = N60,000. Living costs that would be paid anyway are not included, because they are not caused by the decision. Calling education an investment does not remove its cost.


Question 035. Adam Smith's "The Wealth of Nations" (1776) is often regarded as the starting point of modern economics mainly because it:

A. Gave a systematic account of how markets, division of labour and self-interest create national wealth
B. Introduced the mathematical theory of marginal utility
C. Developed Keynesian theory of unemployment
D. Founded welfare economics using Pareto efficiency

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Smith's book, "An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations", set out a systematic theory of how specialisation, exchange and competitive markets raise productivity and wealth. Marginal utility came in the 1870s (Jevons, Menger, Walras), Keynes's General Theory in 1936, and Pareto efficiency in the early 20th century.


Question 036. Which of the following statements could be tested against evidence and is therefore positive?

A. Income inequality in Nigeria is unacceptable.
B. Government should prioritise education over defence.
C. Higher import tariffs on rice increased the domestic price of rice.
D. Rich people should pay a higher share of tax.

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Whether tariffs raised the domestic price of rice can be checked by examining price data, so it is positive. Options A, B and D contain value judgements ("unacceptable", "should"), which data alone cannot settle.


Question 037. Which feature of human wants makes the economic problem permanent?

A. Wants are fixed and easily satisfied.
B. Wants exist only for luxury goods.
C. Wants are unlimited and recurrent, and new wants appear as old ones are satisfied.
D. Wants are identical for all people.

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Human wants are numerous, many recur (food is needed every day), and as income grows new wants appear (mobile phones, then data, then streaming services). Because wants keep growing while resources remain limited, scarcity and choice never disappear. Wants differ between people, and they include necessities as well as luxuries.


Question 038. A "scale of preference" is:

A. A weighing instrument used in markets
B. A table showing prices of goods
C. A government list of essential commodities
D. A list of a person's wants arranged in order of importance

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Because not all wants can be satisfied, a rational individual ranks wants by urgency and satisfies the most pressing first. The highest-ranked want left unsatisfied when a choice is made represents the opportunity cost of that choice. The scale of preference therefore links scarcity, choice and opportunity cost.


Question 039. A government decides to build either a stadium or a bridge, and chooses the bridge. Which statement correctly identifies the opportunity cost?

A. The money spent on the bridge
B. The stadium that could have been built instead
C. The bridge itself
D. Nothing, because the bridge is useful

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Opportunity cost is the alternative forgone, here the stadium. The money cost of the bridge is a measure of resources used, but the economic idea is what those resources could otherwise have produced. A useful choice still has an opportunity cost.


Question 040. On a production possibility curve, a point outside the curve indicates:

A. Inefficient use of resources
B. A combination that is unattainable with current resources and technology
C. Full employment of resources
D. Economic recession

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: The PPC marks the maximum combinations attainable. Points outside it cannot be produced now, although growth (more resources or better technology) or trade could make them reachable later. Points inside show inefficiency or unemployment; points on the curve show full and efficient use of resources.


Question 041. Which of the following would cause a country's PPC to shift inward?

A. A natural disaster that destroys a large share of the capital stock
B. An increase in the labour force
C. Improved technology in agriculture
D. A rise in investment in machinery

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: An inward shift means productive capacity has fallen. Destroying capital reduces the resources available. More labour, better technology and more investment all increase capacity and shift the curve outward.


Question 042. Technological progress that affects only the production of machines (not food) will cause the PPC to:

A. Shift outward parallel along both axes
B. Remain unchanged
C. Shift inward
D. Pivot outward along the machine axis, with the food intercept unchanged

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: If all resources were used for food, maximum food output would be unchanged, so the food intercept stays the same. Maximum machine output rises, so the curve swings outward along the machine axis. The economy can now produce more machines for any given amount of food, so the improvement benefits combinations of both goods.


Question 043. Economic efficiency in the sense of productive efficiency means:

A. Producing whatever goods consumers want most
B. Dividing income equally among citizens
C. Producing output at the lowest possible cost, so that no more of one good can be made without less of another
D. Producing only goods that are sold at high prices

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Productive efficiency means producing with the least resources possible, which places the economy on its PPC. Allocative efficiency (Option A, roughly) means choosing the point on the PPC that best matches society's preferences. Equal distribution concerns equity, not efficiency.


Question 044. Which of the following is the best example of a value judgement disguised as a positive statement?

A. The excessive profits of oil companies harm the economy.
B. Unemployment fell by 2 percentage points last year.
C. A tax on sugary drinks raised their retail price.
D. Exports of cocoa rose in 2025.

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: "Excessive" is a value judgement: it assumes a standard of what profits should be. The statement sounds factual, but deciding what is excessive is normative. "Harm the economy" is also vague and loaded. Options B, C and D are testable, factual claims.


Question 045. The term "economy" in "economics" originally comes from the Greek "oikonomia", which referred to:

A. International trade
B. The study of money
C. Government taxation
D. The management of a household

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: "Oikonomia" combines "oikos" (house) and "nomos" (law or management), meaning household management. The idea of managing limited resources carefully carries over to modern economics, which studies the allocation of scarce resources by households, firms and nations.


Question 046. A farmer has 10 hectares of land. Each hectare can produce 4 tonnes of cassava or 2 tonnes of groundnuts. If he moves from 10 hectares of cassava to 6 hectares of cassava and 4 of groundnuts, he gains:

A. 4 tonnes of groundnuts and gives up 8 tonnes of cassava
B. 8 tonnes of groundnuts and gives up 16 tonnes of cassava
C. 8 tonnes of groundnuts and gives up 8 tonnes of cassava
D. 16 tonnes of groundnuts and gives up 8 tonnes of cassava

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Moving 4 hectares to groundnuts produces 4 x 2 = 8 tonnes of groundnuts and loses 4 x 4 = 16 tonnes of cassava. The opportunity cost of 1 tonne of groundnuts is 16 / 8 = 2 tonnes of cassava, which matches the per-hectare ratio of 4 to 2.


Question 047. Economics is related to sociology because both:

A. Use identical mathematical models
B. Deal only with prices
C. Ignore human behaviour
D. Study human behaviour in society, and economic decisions are influenced by social norms, class and institutions

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Sociology studies social groups, institutions and norms, all of which affect economic choices such as consumption, marriage payments, saving in cooperative societies and labour participation. Economists increasingly draw on these insights, for example in institutional and behavioural economics.


Question 048. Ethics is related to economics mainly through:

A. Positive analysis of market prices
B. The use of statistics
C. The measurement of gross domestic product
D. Normative economics, which involves judgements about fairness and what ought to be

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Ethics deals with right and wrong. Normative economics asks questions such as whether income should be redistributed or whether a policy is just, and these require ethical judgements. Positive economics tries to describe and explain without such judgements.


Question 049. Which of the following best explains why economics is considered a social science rather than a natural science?

A. It studies human behaviour and institutions, where controlled experiments are often difficult.
B. It never uses data.
C. It deals only with opinions.
D. It has no theories.

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Economics studies people, whose behaviour responds to expectations, institutions and incentives. Controlled laboratory experiments are usually harder than in physics or chemistry, although experimental economics exists. Economics still builds theories and tests them with data, so Options B, C and D are false.


Question 050. A worker earns N5,000 per hour. She spends 3 hours queuing to buy fuel at a controlled price of N600 per litre, buying 20 litres, instead of buying instantly at N800 per litre from a private seller. What is the effective cost per litre of the queued fuel, counting her time?

A. N600
B. N750
C. N1,350
D. N800

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Money cost = 20 x N600 = N12,000. Time cost = 3 x N5,000 = N15,000. Total = N27,000. Cost per litre = N27,000 / 20 = N1,350. That is more than the N800 private price, so queuing is the worse choice for her. Price controls can raise the real cost of goods once the time spent obtaining them is counted.


Question 051. Specialisation of countries according to comparative advantage requires which basic condition?

A. Each country must have an absolute advantage in every good.
B. Countries must have different opportunity costs of producing goods.
C. Countries must have identical resources.
D. Trade must be banned.

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: David Ricardo showed that trade benefits both countries as long as their opportunity costs differ, even if one country is better at producing everything (has an absolute advantage in all goods). If opportunity costs were identical, neither country would gain by specialising.


Question 052. Wealth, in the economic sense, refers to:

A. The flow of income received per month
B. Only cash held by individuals
C. The stock of assets with market value owned at a point in time
D. The total satisfaction derived from consumption

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Wealth is a stock measured at a point in time (houses, land, shares, savings). Income is a flow measured over a period (wages per month). A person can have high wealth but low income, such as a retired landowner, or the reverse, such as a young professional. Satisfaction is utility.


Question 053. Which of the following best illustrates a stock variable rather than a flow variable?

A. Monthly salary
B. Annual national output
C. The amount of money in your bank account on 31 December
D. Daily production of crude oil

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: A stock is measured at a point in time; a flow is measured per unit of time. A bank balance on a specific date is a stock. Salary per month, output per year and production per day are all flows. Confusing stocks and flows is a common error in economic reasoning.


Question 054. A wealthy country still faces scarcity because:

A. Its government prints too little money
B. Scarcity applies only to individuals
C. It produces only free goods
D. Its resources, including time, are still limited relative to its wants

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: High income does not remove the gap between wants and resources. Wealthy countries still make trade-offs between healthcare, defence and infrastructure, and individuals still have only 24 hours a day. Printing money creates no real resources and tends to cause inflation.


Question 055. Which of the following is an example of "how to produce" decisions?

A. Whether to harvest rice manually with labour or with combine harvesters
B. Whether to build schools or hospitals
C. Whether rich or poor households receive more goods
D. Whether to produce more cars or more trucks

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: "How to produce" concerns the choice of technique, meaning the combination of factors. Labour-intensive versus capital-intensive harvesting is such a choice, usually guided by relative factor prices. Options B and D are "what to produce", and Option C is "for whom".


Question 056. In a market economy, "consumer sovereignty" means:

A. Consumer spending decisions ultimately determine what goods are produced.
B. Consumers elect the government.
C. Consumers set prices by law.
D. Firms ignore consumer wishes.

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Consumers "vote" with their spending. Goods that people buy are profitable and are produced; goods nobody wants make losses and disappear. Critics note that advertising, monopoly power and unequal incomes limit consumer sovereignty in practice, because people with more money have more "votes".


Question 057. Which of the following is a weakness of the free-market system?

A. It may under-provide public goods and allow large inequality of income.
B. It provides no incentives for efficiency.
C. It always leads to shortages.
D. It requires a central planning agency.

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Markets give strong incentives for efficiency, but they may fail to provide public goods such as street lighting (non-excludable), may ignore externalities like pollution, and can produce wide income inequality. These market failures justify some government role, which is why most economies are mixed. Persistent shortages are more typical of price controls and planning.


Question 058. A student argues: "Water is more useful than diamonds, so water should be more expensive." Economists resolve this paradox of value by pointing out that:

A. Prices are random.
B. Water has no utility.
C. Diamonds are free goods.
D. Price depends on marginal utility and scarcity, not total utility.

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Adam Smith noted the "diamond-water paradox". Later marginalist economists explained it: water has high total utility, but because it is plentiful the marginal utility of an extra litre is low. Diamonds are scarce, so the marginal utility of one more is high. Price reflects value at the margin, not total usefulness.


Question 059. Which of the following would NOT be a factor of production?

A. A tractor used on a farm
B. A computer programmer writing software for a firm
C. A forest used for timber
D. A share certificate in a manufacturing company

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: A share certificate is a claim on a company's profits, a financial asset, not an input into production. The tractor is capital, the programmer supplies labour, and the forest is land.


Question 060. Mobility of factors of production refers to:

A. The ease with which factors can move between occupations or locations
B. The speed at which goods are transported
C. The number of firms in an industry
D. The rate of inflation

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Labour can be occupationally mobile (changing jobs) or geographically mobile (changing location). Land is geographically immobile but can switch uses, for example from farming to housing. Low factor mobility slows adjustment to changes in demand and can cause structural unemployment.


Question 061. Which statement about "free goods" is correct?

A. Free goods have an opportunity cost.
B. Free goods are always man-made.
C. All goods provided by government are free goods.
D. A good that is free in one place may be an economic good in another.

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Whether a good is free depends on circumstances. Sand in the Sahara is plentiful and free, but sand delivered to a building site in Lagos requires digging and transport, making it an economic good. Free goods have no opportunity cost, are usually gifts of nature, and are different from goods the government provides at no charge.


Question 062. Which concept best explains why a firm chooses to produce cement instead of roofing sheets with its factory?

A. Scale of preference and opportunity cost
B. Fallacy of composition
C. Inflation
D. Free goods

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: The firm ranks its alternatives (its "scale of preference" as a producer) by expected returns and chooses the best. The profit it gives up from roofing sheets is the opportunity cost of producing cement. This is the same scarcity-choice-opportunity-cost logic that applies to consumers.


Question 063. Economics can best be described as:

A. A study of money only
B. A study of government only
C. A study of business accounting
D. A study of how individuals and societies use scarce resources to satisfy wants

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Economics is far broader than money or business. It covers choices by households, firms and governments about how to use limited resources, including time, labour and natural resources. Money is one tool within the economy; accounting records transactions; government is one actor among many.


Question 064. A shopkeeper who uses her own building for her shop instead of renting it out for N200,000 a year incurs:

A. No cost, because she owns the building
B. A sunk cost that should be ignored
C. An explicit cost of N200,000 a year
D. An implicit cost of N200,000 a year

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Implicit costs are the opportunity costs of using resources the owner already has. By using the building herself she gives up N200,000 in rent. No money changes hands, so it is not explicit, but it is a real economic cost. The building's original purchase price is a sunk cost; the forgone rent is not.


Question 065. Which of the following would most likely move an economy from a point inside its PPC to a point on it?

A. Discovery of new mineral deposits
B. Improved technology
C. A rise in population
D. Reduction of unemployment through better use of existing resources

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: A point inside the curve shows unused or wasted resources. Putting those existing resources to work moves the economy to the curve without shifting it. New deposits, population growth and better technology shift the curve itself outward.


Question 066. Which statement about the relationship between economics and mathematics is most accurate?

A. Economics is a branch of mathematics.
B. Mathematics has no use in economics.
C. Mathematics provides tools such as equations and graphs that help economists state and analyse relationships precisely.
D. Mathematical results replace the need for economic reasoning.

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Economists use algebra (demand equations), calculus (marginal analysis) and graphs (supply and demand diagrams) to state theories precisely and derive conclusions. Mathematics is a tool; the economic content comes from assumptions about behaviour and institutions. Mathematical economics and econometrics developed from this relationship.


Question 067. Economics is related to history because:

A. History provides past data and episodes, such as depressions and inflations, that help test and develop economic theories.
B. Economists write political biographies.
C. History predicts future prices exactly.
D. Economic theories never change over time.

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Economic history provides evidence, for example the Great Depression, which influenced Keynes, or hyperinflation episodes. Economic ideas themselves developed historically (classical, neoclassical and Keynesian schools). History does not give exact forecasts, and theories do change as evidence accumulates.


Question 068. Which of the following best describes economic growth as shown on a PPC diagram?

A. A movement along the PPC
B. An outward shift of the PPC
C. A movement from on the PPC to inside it
D. A change in the slope of the PPC only at one point

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Economic growth is an increase in productive capacity, shown by an outward shift of the PPC, which makes previously unattainable combinations possible. A movement along the curve reallocates resources between goods, and a move inside the curve reflects a recession or unemployment.


Question 069. A country devotes more resources to capital goods today rather than consumer goods. The likely consequence is:

A. Higher consumption today and slower growth in future
B. Lower consumption today but faster growth of the PPC in future
C. No change in future production possibilities
D. An inward shift of the PPC in future

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Capital goods (machines, infrastructure) raise future productive capacity. The opportunity cost is the consumer goods given up today. Choosing more capital now therefore means sacrificing current consumption for a larger outward shift of the PPC later, an intertemporal trade-off.


Question 070. Which of the following is the most accurate description of "rational behaviour" in economics?

A. People always behave selfishly.
B. People make choices that they expect to best achieve their objectives, given their constraints and information.
C. People never make mistakes.
D. People always choose the cheapest product.

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Rationality in economics means acting consistently to pursue one's goals given constraints, comparing costs and benefits. Goals can include caring for others. Rational people can still make mistakes with imperfect information, and the cheapest product is not always the best value.


Question 071. Marginal analysis means making decisions by comparing:

A. Total benefits with total costs only
B. Average costs across firms
C. The additional benefit and additional cost of a small change in an activity
D. Past costs with future costs

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: "Marginal" means additional or incremental. A rational decision-maker expands an activity as long as the marginal benefit of one more unit exceeds its marginal cost, and stops where they are equal. Marginal thinking is central to modern economics, from consumer choice to profit maximisation.


Question 072. A student has already paid N15,000 for a non-refundable concert ticket. On the day, she feels ill and would rather stay home. According to economic reasoning she should:

A. Go, because she has already paid N15,000
B. Go, because sunk costs must always be recovered
C. Sell her phone to recover the N15,000
D. Decide by comparing the benefit and cost of going now, ignoring the N15,000 already spent

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: The N15,000 is a sunk cost: it cannot be recovered whatever she does, so it should not affect her decision. She should compare the benefit of attending against the cost of going while ill. Letting sunk costs drive decisions is known as the sunk-cost fallacy.


Question 073. Which of the following best illustrates an incentive?

A. The rising of the sun
B. A fall in the price of bread that leads households to buy more bread
C. A fixed rainfall pattern
D. A historical fact that cannot change

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: An incentive is something that motivates people to act, often a change in costs or benefits. A lower price reduces the cost of bread, encouraging more purchases. Economics predicts behaviour largely by analysing how people respond to incentives.


Question 074. Division of labour within a single bakery, where one worker mixes dough, another bakes and another packs, increases productivity mainly because:

A. Workers are paid less.
B. Workers become more skilled at a narrow task and time spent switching between tasks is saved.
C. Fewer machines are used.
D. Demand for bread falls.

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Adam Smith's famous pin-factory example gave three reasons: greater skill through repetition, time saved by not switching tasks, and easier invention of specialised machines. Disadvantages include monotony, dependence on other workers and the risk of unemployment if a single skill becomes obsolete.


Question 075. Which of the following is the major disadvantage of specialisation for an individual worker?

A. Monotony of work and vulnerability if demand for that specific skill falls
B. Higher productivity
C. More output per hour
D. Easier use of machines

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Doing one narrow task repeatedly can be boring and reduce job satisfaction. A worker with a single skill also faces a higher risk of unemployment if technology or demand changes. Options B, C and D are advantages of specialisation.


Question 076. Which exchange system requires a "double coincidence of wants"?

A. Money economy
B. Mobile money transfers
C. Credit card payments
D. Barter

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: In barter, goods are exchanged directly for goods. A trade happens only if each party wants what the other offers, which is the double coincidence of wants. Money removes this problem by serving as a generally accepted medium of exchange, making specialisation and trade far easier.


Question 077. Which pair of terms is correctly matched?

A. Positive economics - what is, was or will be
B. Normative economics - what is
C. Positive economics - what ought to be
D. Normative economics - testable predictions

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Positive economics deals with factual statements about what is, was or will be, which can in principle be tested. Normative economics deals with what ought to be, based on value judgements. Options B, C and D swap these definitions.


Question 078. Which of the following is NOT a valid reason why economics is studied?

A. To understand how resources are allocated
B. To guarantee that everyone becomes rich
C. To help government design better policies
D. To help individuals make better decisions

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Economics improves understanding of choices, markets and policy, and it helps individuals, firms and governments make better decisions. It cannot guarantee wealth for anyone; scarcity means trade-offs remain.


Question 079. If the opportunity cost of producing one more unit of good X increases as more X is produced, the PPC will be:

A. A straight line
B. Convex to the origin (bowed inward)
C. Concave to the origin (bowed outward)
D. Vertical

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Increasing opportunity cost makes the PPC steeper as more X is produced (with X on the horizontal axis), so it bows outward. Constant cost gives a straight line, and decreasing cost would give a curve bowed inward.


Question 080. Two countries, A and B, can each produce cloth and wheat. In country A, 1 cloth costs 2 wheat; in country B, 1 cloth costs 4 wheat. Which country has the comparative advantage in cloth?

A. Both equally
B. Country B
C. Country A
D. Neither, since comparative advantage requires absolute advantage

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Comparative advantage goes to the country with the lower opportunity cost. Cloth costs 2 wheat in A and 4 wheat in B, so A has the comparative advantage in cloth. B therefore has the comparative advantage in wheat (1 wheat costs 1/4 cloth in B versus 1/2 cloth in A). Absolute advantage is not required.


Question 081. Which of these is a flow concept?

A. A nation's capital stock
B. A firm's investment spending during the year
C. A household's savings balance at year-end
D. The land area of Nigeria

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Investment is spending per period, which is a flow. It adds to the capital stock (Option A), which is measured at a point in time. Bank balances and land area are also stocks.


Question 082. Which statement best describes the difference between "needs" and "wants" as used in introductory economics?

A. They are identical and cannot be distinguished.
B. Needs are unlimited, while wants are limited.
C. Needs are basic requirements for survival, while wants are broader desires that go beyond survival.
D. Wants apply only to the poor.

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Needs, such as food, shelter and clothing, are essential for survival. Wants include all goods and services people desire, many beyond basic needs. Economics treats wants overall as unlimited, which together with limited resources produces scarcity.


Question 083. The study of economics helps a government most directly in:

A. Analysing the likely effects of policies such as taxes, subsidies and interest rates
B. Predicting weather patterns
C. Designing military uniforms
D. Determining religious doctrine

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Economic analysis predicts how taxes, spending, regulation and monetary policy affect prices, output, employment and welfare. This guides policy design. The other options fall outside economics.


Question 084. "Ceteris paribus" means:

A. All things change together
B. All other things being equal
C. In the long run
D. Profit maximisation

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Ceteris paribus is Latin for "other things being equal". Economists hold other influences constant in order to isolate the relationship between two variables, for example price and quantity demanded. It is a key tool of economic method, discussed further in Chapter 2.


Question 085. A country moves along its PPC from producing more consumer goods to producing more military goods. This movement illustrates:

A. Economic growth
B. A shift of the PPC
C. Unemployment
D. Opportunity cost, since more military goods require fewer consumer goods

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Along the PPC, resources are fully employed, so more of one good requires less of the other. This trade-off, often called "guns versus butter", is the opportunity cost of the choice. The curve itself does not move.


Question 086. Which of the following best describes "utility" in introductory economics?

A. The usefulness of a good as judged by government
B. The cost of producing a good
C. The price of a good
D. The satisfaction a consumer derives from consuming a good or service

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Utility is the satisfaction or benefit a person gets from consumption. It is subjective: the same good can give different people different utility. It is not the same as price or cost, as the diamond-water paradox shows.


Question 087. Which description of the relationship between scarcity and choice is correct?

A. Scarcity makes choice necessary, and every choice involves an opportunity cost.
B. Choice causes scarcity.
C. Scarcity and choice are unrelated.
D. Choice eliminates scarcity.

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Because resources are limited relative to wants, people must choose among alternatives. Each choice gives up the next best alternative, its opportunity cost. Choice does not remove scarcity; it is the way people respond to it.


Question 088. An economy produces only two goods, X and Y. Its PPC is a straight line from 60 X to 30 Y. Currently it produces 20 X. Assuming full employment, how much Y does it produce?

A. 10
B. 40
C. 30
D. 20

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: The straight line runs from (60 X, 0 Y) to (0 X, 30 Y), so each unit of X costs 30/60 = 0.5 Y. Producing 20 X gives up 20 x 0.5 = 10 Y from the maximum of 30. Y = 30 - 10 = 20. Check: Y = 30 - 0.5(20) = 20.


Question 089. Economics is related to geography mainly because:

A. Location, climate and natural resources affect what and how countries produce and trade.
B. Geography determines all prices.
C. Economists draw maps of rivers.
D. They study identical subject matter.

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: The availability of land, minerals and climate influences production patterns; for example, cocoa grows in the south-west of Nigeria and groundnuts in the north. Distance affects transport costs and trade. Regional and urban economics combine both disciplines.


Question 090. Which of the following is a feature of capital as a factor of production?

A. It is a free gift of nature.
B. It earns wages.
C. Its supply is perfectly fixed.
D. It is subject to depreciation through wear and tear.

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Capital goods wear out or become obsolete over time, which is called depreciation. Society must invest to replace them. Gifts of nature with fixed supply describe land, and wages are the reward of labour.


Question 091. A society chooses to allocate a large share of output to the elderly through pensions. This is primarily a decision about:

A. What to produce
B. How to produce
C. For whom to produce
D. Whether scarcity exists

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Pensions determine how output is distributed among people, which is the "for whom" question. They do not directly change what is produced or the production techniques used.


Question 092. Which of the following best explains why opportunity cost is important even for decisions that do not involve money?

A. It applies only to financial transactions.
B. It applies only to firms.
C. It applies to any decision involving scarce resources, including time and effort.
D. It is irrelevant to individuals.

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Spending an evening watching football has an opportunity cost (studying, working or resting), even if no money is spent. Opportunity cost applies whenever a scarce resource with alternative uses is allocated, which covers time, attention and effort as well as money.


Question 093. Which of the following would economists regard as an "economic resource"?

A. An idle worker
B. Useless desert sand far from any market
C. Air in the open atmosphere
D. A desire for a new car

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: An unemployed worker is still a scarce resource with productive potential; unemployment means the resource is wasted. Plentiful desert sand and open air are free goods. A desire is a want, not a resource.


Question 094. If a country's PPC between food and clothing is a straight line, the opportunity cost of clothing in terms of food is:

A. Constant
B. Decreasing
C. Increasing
D. Zero

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: A straight-line PPC has a constant slope, meaning each additional unit of clothing requires giving up the same amount of food. This happens when resources are equally suited to both goods.


Question 095. Which of the following is most likely to be studied in macroeconomics?

A. The pricing policy of a telecommunications company
B. The causes of a fall in Nigeria's real gross domestic product
C. The demand for tomatoes in Kano
D. The wage of a carpenter

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Real GDP measures the total output of the whole economy, so changes in it are macroeconomic. The other options concern individual firms, markets or workers, which belong to microeconomics.


Question 096. Which statement correctly describes the relationship between microeconomics and macroeconomics?

A. They are entirely separate and never interact.
B. Macroeconomics is simply microeconomics multiplied by the population.
C. Macroeconomic outcomes result from many individual decisions, and macro conditions affect individual decisions.
D. Microeconomics deals only with poor people.

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Aggregate output, inflation and unemployment emerge from the decisions of millions of households and firms, and macro conditions such as recessions and interest rates shape those decisions. Because of the fallacy of composition, macroeconomics cannot be obtained by simply scaling up individual behaviour, so Option B is wrong.


Question 097. A woman can earn N20,000 a day as a tailor or N12,000 a day as a trader. She chooses to work as a tailor. If the government imposes a tax that reduces tailoring income to N10,000 a day, rational behaviour predicts that she will:

A. Remain a tailor, since she has already chosen
B. Demand a subsidy
C. Stop working altogether
D. Switch to trading, because its return (N12,000) now exceeds tailoring (N10,000)

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Before the tax, the opportunity cost of tailoring was N12,000 and its return N20,000, so tailoring was best. After the tax, tailoring yields N10,000, less than the N12,000 from trading. If other aspects of the two jobs are similar, she will switch. Changes in relative rewards change choices; this is how incentives reallocate resources.


Question 098. Which of the following is NOT an assumption normally made when drawing a production possibility curve?

A. Resources are fixed in quantity.
B. Technology is given.
C. The economy produces an unlimited number of goods.
D. Resources are fully and efficiently employed.

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: The simple PPC assumes the economy produces only two goods (or groups of goods), with fixed resources and technology, fully and efficiently employed, over a given period. The two-good assumption keeps the analysis simple enough to draw on a graph.


Question 099. Which of the following best summarises the concept of "trade-off"?

A. Getting more of everything at once
B. Paying taxes to government
C. Exchanging goods across borders only
D. Giving up some of one thing in order to get more of another

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: A trade-off arises when resources are scarce: getting more of one thing means having less of another. The value of what is given up is the opportunity cost. Trade-offs apply to individuals (leisure versus income), firms and governments (education versus defence spending).


Question 100. (Revision) A government considers subsidising fertiliser. An economist says: "The subsidy will raise maize output by 15% but will cost N50 billion, which could otherwise fund 200 new schools. Whether this is worthwhile depends on society's priorities." Which combination of concepts does this statement best illustrate?

A. Free goods and the fallacy of composition
B. Positive analysis, opportunity cost and normative judgement
C. Barter and double coincidence of wants
D. Absolute advantage and depreciation

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: The predictions about maize output and cost are positive (testable). The 200 forgone schools are the opportunity cost of the subsidy. The final remark, that the choice depends on society's priorities, recognises that a normative value judgement is needed to decide. The statement brings together the main ideas of Chapter 1.

Question 101. Which reasoning method moves from general assumptions to specific conclusions?

A. Inductive reasoning
B. Deductive reasoning
C. Random sampling
D. Empirical observation

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Deduction starts from premises (e.g. firms maximise profit) and derives specific predictions logically.


Question 102. Inductive reasoning in economics involves:

A. Generalising from observed data and particular cases
B. Deriving conclusions purely from axioms
C. Ignoring evidence
D. Making value judgements

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Induction builds general statements from repeated observation, e.g. noticing that sales fall whenever prices rise.


Question 103. An economic model is best described as:

A. A government policy document
B. An exact copy of the real economy
C. A physical machine
D. A simplified representation of reality used to explain and predict

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Models deliberately abstract from detail so the key relationships can be studied.


Question 104. Economists use simplifying assumptions mainly because:

A. They isolate the essential relationships in a complex world
B. They avoid the need for data
C. They guarantee correct predictions
D. They make models completely realistic

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Abstraction trades realism for clarity; a model is judged by how useful its predictions are.


Question 105. A hypothesis in economics is:

A. A testable proposition about the relationship between variables
B. A proven law that never fails
C. A statistical table
D. A value judgement

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: A hypothesis must be capable of being confirmed or rejected by evidence.


Question 106. Milton Friedman (1953) argued that an economic theory should be judged mainly by:

A. The number of equations used
B. The realism of its assumptions
C. The accuracy of its predictions
D. Its political popularity

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: In "The Methodology of Positive Economics", Friedman stressed predictive power over realism of assumptions.


Question 107. Economic laws differ from laws in physics mainly because they:

A. Are made by parliament
B. Never have exceptions
C. Express tendencies that hold on average, ceteris paribus
D. Cannot be stated in words

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Human behaviour varies, so economic laws are statements of probable tendency, not certainties.


Question 108. The main purpose of the ceteris paribus assumption is to:

A. Make all variables change together
B. Remove the need for theory
C. Isolate the effect of one variable by holding others constant
D. Measure inflation

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Holding other influences constant lets us study, for example, price and quantity demanded alone.


Question 109. Ice cream sales and drowning incidents rise together each summer. The most valid conclusion is that:

A. Ice cream causes drowning
B. Drowning causes ice cream sales
C. Both may be caused by a third factor, hot weather
D. The data are false

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Correlation does not prove causation; a common cause (temperature) can drive both.


Question 110. An endogenous variable is one that:

A. Is fixed outside the model
B. Is always a price
C. Never changes
D. Is determined within the model

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Endogenous variables (e.g. equilibrium price) are explained by the model; exogenous ones are taken as given.


Question 111. In a model of the maize market, rainfall would usually be treated as:

A. An endogenous variable
B. A constant that never varies
C. An exogenous variable
D. A dependent variable explained by price

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Rainfall affects the market but is not determined by maize prices, so it is exogenous.


Question 112. In the demand function Qd = 100 - 2P, the dependent variable is:

A. The number 2
B. Quantity demanded
C. Price
D. The number 100

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Qd depends on P; price is the independent variable.


Question 113. A "post hoc ergo propter hoc" error means:

A. Assuming the whole equals its parts
B. Holding variables constant
C. Assuming that because B followed A, A caused B
D. Ignoring sunk costs

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Sequence in time does not by itself establish causation.


Question 114. Empirical analysis in economics refers to:

A. Expressing personal opinions
B. Drawing diagrams only
C. Writing purely logical proofs
D. Testing theories against real-world data

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Empirical work uses observation and data, often through econometrics.


Question 115. Which statement is prescriptive?

A. The central bank should cut interest rates to boost growth.
B. Lower interest rates usually increase borrowing.
C. Interest rates were cut last year.
D. Inflation was 20% in 2024.

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Prescriptive (normative) statements recommend actions; the others describe or predict.


Question 116. A descriptive statement in economics:

A. Expresses moral approval
B. Cannot be checked
C. Reports facts about what is or has been
D. Recommends a policy

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Description records facts, such as the unemployment rate in a given year.


Question 117. Which is a major limitation of economic analysis?

A. Economists never use data
B. Economics has no theories
C. All economic statements are normative
D. Controlled experiments are difficult because many factors change at once

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Real economies change simultaneously in many ways, making causes hard to isolate.


Question 118. The slope of a straight-line graph from (0, 10) to (5, 0) is:

A. -2
B. -0.5
C. 2
D. 10

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Slope = change in Y / change in X = (0 - 10)/(5 - 0) = -2.


Question 119. Two variables are inversely related if:

A. Neither changes
B. One rises when the other falls
C. They are unrelated
D. Both rise together

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: An inverse (negative) relationship gives a downward-sloping line, as with price and quantity demanded.


Question 120. A time-series dataset records:

A. Values across many units at one time
B. Only qualitative opinions
C. Values of a variable over successive periods
D. Hypothetical numbers

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Time-series data, e.g. annual GDP 2000-2025, track one unit over time; cross-section data compare units at one time.


Question 121. Data on household incomes in 36 states collected in one survey year are:

A. Time-series data
B. Cross-sectional data
C. Panel data over decades
D. Experimental data

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Cross-section data compare different units at a single point in time.


Question 122. The "positive" approach to economics was emphasised in the writings of John Neville Keynes (1891), who distinguished:

A. Micro and macroeconomics
B. Barter and money
C. Positive science, normative science and the art of economics
D. Classical and Keynesian theory

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: J. N. Keynes's "Scope and Method of Political Economy" separated what is, what ought to be, and practical rules.


Question 123. A model predicts that raising the price of bread reduces sales. Bread sales fall after a price rise. This evidence:

A. Supports the hypothesis but does not prove it absolutely
B. Is irrelevant
C. Proves the theory beyond doubt
D. Disproves the theory

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Evidence can be consistent with a theory; other factors may also explain the change.


Question 124. Karl Popper's idea of "falsifiability" implies a scientific economic theory must:

A. Contain no assumptions
B. Be capable of being shown false by evidence
C. Be impossible to test
D. Be accepted by everyone

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: A theory that no conceivable evidence could contradict is not scientific in Popper's sense.


Question 125. Which best describes an economic "theory"?

A. A set of related hypotheses explaining how economic phenomena work
B. A single observed fact
C. A government statute
D. A random guess

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Theories combine assumptions and hypotheses into a coherent explanation that yields predictions.


Question 126. The method of "successive approximation" in economics means:

A. Starting with a simple model and gradually relaxing assumptions
B. Using only real data
C. Ignoring assumptions entirely

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Economists begin with simple cases (e.g. perfect competition) then add realistic complications.


Question 127. A flow chart of income between households and firms is an example of:

A. A production function
B. A circular flow model

D. A demand schedule

Explanation: The circular flow model shows factor services flowing to firms and goods flowing to households, with money moving the other way.


Question 128. Which is an example of a value judgement entering positive analysis?

A. Counting unemployed persons

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Even positive economics involves value-laden choices about what to research.


Question 129. Which is TRUE of economic predictions?

A. They are always exact
B. They cannot be tested
C. They never depend on data

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Predictions take the form "if X, then Y, other things equal".


Question 130. Static analysis in economics:

A. Ignores equilibrium
B. Studies equilibrium at a point without tracing the path of change
C. Studies changes over time step by step

Question 131. Comparative static analysis compares:

A. Normative and positive views
B. Prices of two goods at one moment

Question 132. Dynamic analysis focuses on:

A. A single snapshot equilibrium
B. Value judgements

Explanation: Dynamic analysis studies how variables move over time, e.g. the cobweb model.


Question 133. Partial equilibrium analysis differs from general equilibrium analysis because it:

A. Ignores prices
B. Is used only in macroeconomics

Question 134. General equilibrium analysis is most associated with:

A. Thomas Malthus
B. Adam Smith

Question 135. A normative statement can be distinguished by:

Question 136. A "stylised fact" in economics is:

Question 138. Economic analysis that uses mathematical equations to express theory is called:

Question 139. Econometrics combines:

Question 140. Which situation best illustrates the fallacy of composition?

Question 141. A deductive economic argument is valid if:

Question 142. A key weakness of purely inductive reasoning is that:

Question 143. The "rational economic man" assumption states that individuals:

Question 201. Classical economics is generally dated from:

A. Adam Smith's Wealth of Nations (1776) to around John Stuart Mill (c. 1870s)
B. 1950 to the present
C. 1500 to 1600
D. The Great Depression of the 1930s

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: The classical period runs from Smith through Ricardo, Malthus, Say and J. S. Mill, before the marginal revolution of the 1870s.


Question 202. The economic doctrine that classical economists most directly criticised was:

A. Mercantilism
B. Marginalism
C. Keynesianism
D. Monetarism

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Mercantilists equated wealth with gold and favoured export surpluses and protection; Smith attacked these views.


Question 203. Mercantilists believed a nation's wealth consisted mainly of:

A. The welfare of consumers
B. Its land area alone
C. The productivity of its labour
D. Its stock of gold and silver

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Smith argued instead that wealth is the annual produce of land and labour.


Question 204. The Physiocrats, led by François Quesnay, held that the only truly productive sector was:

A. Manufacturing
B. Banking
C. Trade
D. Agriculture

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Physiocrats saw land as the sole source of net product (produit net); manufacturing was "sterile".


Question 205. Quesnay's Tableau Économique (1758) is significant as an early:

A. Theory of money supply
B. Model of monopoly
C. Representation of the circular flow of income between sectors
D. Theory of marginal utility

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: It traced flows between farmers, landlords and artisans, anticipating circular flow analysis.


Question 206. The phrase "laissez-faire" is most associated with:

A. Minimal government interference in economic affairs
B. High tariffs
C. Central planning
D. Nationalisation of industry

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Coined by French writers and adopted by classical liberals favouring free markets.


Question 207. Adam Smith's other major work, published in 1759, was:

A. The Theory of Moral Sentiments
B. Principles of Political Economy and Taxation
C. Principles of Economics
D. Essay on the Principle of Population

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: In it Smith discussed sympathy and moral judgement, showing he did not see people as purely selfish.


Question 208. Smith's "invisible hand" describes how:

Question 209. Smith stated that "the division of labour is limited by":

B. Governments fix rent

D. Landlords are greedy

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Differential rent equals the surplus of better land over the marginal (no-rent) land.


Question 221. In Ricardo's theory, the marginal land under cultivation earns:

A. The highest rent
B. Average rent
C. Negative rent
D. No rent

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Rent on other land equals its output advantage over the marginal land.


Question 222. Plots A, B and C yield 100, 80 and 60 bags with equal labour and capital. If C is the marginal land, rent on A is:

A. 60 bags
B. 100 bags
C. 40 bags
D. 20 bags

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Rent on A = 100 - 60 = 40; rent on B = 80 - 60 = 20; C earns none.


Question 223. According to Ricardo, a rise in the price of corn is the cause rather than the effect of:

A. High rents
B. Free trade
C. Low wages
D. Falling population

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: "Corn is not high because a rent is paid, but a rent is paid because corn is high."


Question 224. Ricardo opposed the Corn Laws because they:

A. Encouraged imports
B. Raised food prices and rents, squeezing profits and accumulation
C. Raised profits
D. Lowered rents

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Cheap imported corn would lower wages (in money terms) and raise profits for investment.


Question 225. Ricardo's comparative advantage example involved:

A. France and Spain trading silk
B. England and Portugal trading cloth and wine
C. Nigeria and Ghana trading cocoa
D. Scotland and Ireland trading wool

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Portugal had absolute advantage in both, yet both gained by specialising.


Question 226. In Ricardo's example, Portugal needs 90 labour units for cloth and 80 for wine; England needs 100 and 120. Portugal has comparative advantage in:

A. Neither
B. Both equally
C. Cloth
D. Wine

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Portugal's wine/cloth cost ratio 80/90 = 0.89 is lower than England's 120/100 = 1.2.


Question 227. In the same example, England has comparative advantage in:

A. Cloth
B. Both
C. Wine
D. Neither

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: England's cloth costs 100/120 = 0.83 wine, versus 90/80 = 1.125 wine in Portugal.


Question 228. Ricardo argued profits tend to fall over time mainly because:

A. Machines become cheaper
B. Cultivation of poorer land raises food costs, wages and rents
C. Trade increases
D. Population falls

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Diminishing returns on land squeeze profits, moving the economy towards a stationary state.


Question 229. The "stationary state" in classical economics refers to:

A. A planned economy
B. Full employment with growth
C. A stable price level
D. A condition where accumulation and growth cease

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Falling profits remove the incentive to accumulate capital.


Question 230. Ricardian equivalence (a later name) relates to Ricardo's discussion of whether:

A. Labour is the source of value
B. Financing government spending by debt or taxes makes a difference
C. Trade is beneficial
D. Rent equals wages

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Ricardo considered the equivalence but doubted people behaved that way in practice.


Question 231. Ricardo's chapter "On Machinery" (3rd edition, 1821) conceded that:

A. Machinery has no effect on employment
B. Introducing machinery could harm the labouring class, at least temporarily
C. Machines should be banned
D. Machines always benefit workers immediately

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: He revised his earlier view, recognising possible technological unemployment.


Question 232. Thomas Robert Malthus published "An Essay on the Principle of Population" first in:

A. 1776
B. 1817
C. 1848
D. 1798

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Malthus revised it in later editions, adding moral restraint as a check.


Question 233. Malthus argued that, unchecked, population grows:

B. At the same rate as food
C. Not at all
D. Geometrically (1, 2, 4, 8...)

Question 234. Malthus argued that food supply grows:

A. Geometrically
B. Not at all
C. Arithmetically

Explanation: This contrast underlies the Malthusian trap.


Question 235. Malthus's "positive checks" on population include:

A. Contraception

D. Late marriage

Explanation: Positive checks raise mortality; preventive checks lower births.


Question 236. Malthus's "preventive checks" operate by:

A. Encouraging emigration
B. Increasing the death rate
C. Raising food output
D. Reducing the birth rate, e.g. through delayed marriage

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Later editions emphasised "moral restraint" as a preventive check.


Question 237. A major reason Malthus's prediction did not hold for industrial countries was:

A. Population fell to zero
B. Land became unlimited
C. Wages were fixed by law
D. Technological progress in agriculture and falling birth rates

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Rising productivity and the demographic transition offset his mechanism.


Question 238. Malthus differed from Ricardo by arguing that:

A. Trade is harmful
B. Rent does not exist
C. General gluts (overproduction) caused by deficient demand were possible
D. Say's law always holds

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Their correspondence debated gluts; Keynes later praised Malthus's view.


Question 239. The "iron law of wages" (associated with Lassalle, drawing on Malthus and Ricardo) holds that wages tend toward:

A. Subsistence level in the long run
B. Zero
C. Profit levels
D. Ever-rising levels

Explanation: Higher wages raise population, increasing labour supply and pushing wages back to subsistence.


Question 240. Ricardo's "natural price of labour" is:

A. The wage set by unions

Question 241. The wages-fund doctrine held that:

A. Wages depend on marginal utility
B. Wages depend on a fixed fund of capital set aside to pay workers divided by the number of workers

Question 242. Jean-Baptiste Say is best known for the proposition that:

Question 243. Say's law implies that a general glut of goods is:

Question 244. Which economist argued strongly against Say's law in the 20th century?

Question 245. John Stuart Mill's major economics text, published in 1848, was:

A. Elements of Pure Economics

D. Utilitarianism

Explanation: It was the leading economics textbook for decades.

A. Shift its supply curve left
B. Lower its price necessarily
C. Move along its demand curve
D. Shift its demand curve to the right

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Advertising changes tastes and preferences.


Question 533. A health report linking eggs to heart disease would:

A. Move along the egg demand curve
B. Increase egg supply
C. Shift the demand for eggs to the right
D. Shift the demand for eggs to the left

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Tastes and preferences change.


Question 534. Demand function Qd = 120 - 3P. At P = 20, quantity demanded is:

A. 40
B. 180
C. 60
D. 100

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: 120 - 60 = 60.


Question 535. Using Qd = 120 - 3P, the price at which quantity demanded is zero is:

A. 120
B. 30
C. 40
D. 3

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: 0 = 120 - 3P gives P = 40.


Question 536. If Qd = 200 - 4P and price rises from 20 to 25, quantity demanded:

A. Rises by 20 units
B. Falls by 20 units
C. Falls by 5 units
D. Falls by 100 units

Explanation: Qd: 120 to 100, a fall of 20.


Question 537. Demand Qd = 80 - 2P becomes Qd = 100 - 2P. This represents:

A. A movement along the curve
B. A change in supply
C. A decrease in demand
D. An increase in demand of 20 units at every price

Explanation: The intercept increases; the curve shifts right.


Question 538. Price elasticity of demand measures:

A. The responsiveness of supply
B. The responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in the good's price
C. Income changes
D. The change in price

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: PED = % change in Qd / % change in P.


Question 539. Price rises by 10% and quantity demanded falls by 20%. PED (absolute value) is:

A. 20
B. 0.5
C. 10
D. 2

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: 20/10 = 2, elastic.


Question 540. If PED (absolute) = 0.4, demand is:

A. Elastic
B. Unitary
C. Inelastic
D. Perfectly elastic

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Less than 1 means quantity responds less than proportionately.


Question 541. Price falls from N100 to N80 and quantity demanded rises from 50 to 60 (using initial values). PED is:

A. 1
B. 2
C. 1.5
D. 0.5

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: %ΔQ = 20%; %ΔP = -20%; absolute PED = 1.


Question 542. When demand is price elastic, a price cut will:

A. Leave total revenue unchanged
B. Decrease total revenue
C. Increase total revenue
D. Reduce quantity demanded

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Quantity rises proportionally more than price falls.


Question 543. When demand is price inelastic, a price increase will:

A. Decrease total revenue
B. Reduce revenue to zero
C. Leave revenue unchanged
D. Increase total revenue

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Quantity falls proportionally less than price rises.


Question 544. When demand has unitary elasticity, a price change:

A. Makes revenue zero
B. Doubles revenue
C. Halves revenue
D. Leaves total revenue unchanged

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Percentage changes offset exactly.


Question 545. Price rises from N50 to N60; quantity falls from 100 to 90. Total revenue:

A. Rises from N5,000 to N5,400
B. Falls from N5,000 to N4,500
C. Falls to N5,400
D. Stays N5,000

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: TR = 50x100 = 5,000; 60x90 = 5,400. Demand is inelastic here.


Question 546. Perfectly inelastic demand is shown by:

A. A downward curve
B. An upward curve
C. A vertical demand curve
D. A horizontal demand curve

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Quantity does not respond to price (PED = 0).


Question 547. Perfectly elastic demand is shown by:

A. A vertical demand curve
B. An upward curve
C. A rectangular hyperbola
D. A horizontal demand curve

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Any price rise causes quantity to fall to zero.


Question 548. Which good is likely to have the most price-inelastic demand?

A. Restaurant meals
B. A particular brand of biscuit
C. Foreign holidays
D. Insulin for diabetics

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Necessities with no close substitutes are inelastic.


Question 549. Which factor tends to make demand more price elastic?

A. Being a necessity
B. Many close substitutes
C. Small share of income
D. Habit-forming nature

Explanation: Substitutes let consumers switch easily.


Question 550. Demand tends to be more elastic in the long run because:

A. Goods become necessities
B. Consumers have more time to find substitutes and adjust habits

D. Incomes fall

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Adjustment takes time.


Question 551. Goods that take a tiny fraction of income, like matches, tend to have:

A. Perfectly elastic demand
B. Inelastic demand
C. Negative demand
D. Elastic demand

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Price changes have little effect on budgets.


Question 552. The arc (midpoint) elasticity when price rises from 4 to 6 and quantity falls from 12 to 8 is:

A. 1
B. 2
C. 0.8
D. 0.5

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: %ΔQ = -4/10 = -40%; %ΔP = 2/5 = 40%; absolute PED = 1.


Question 553. Income elasticity of demand measures:

A. Responsiveness to price of substitutes
B. Change in tax
C. Responsiveness of demand to changes in income
D. Responsiveness of supply

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: YED = % change in Qd / % change in income.


Question 554. Income rises by 10% and demand for a good rises by 25%. YED is:

A. 0.4, a necessity
B. 2.5, a luxury
C. 25, normal
D. -2.5, inferior

Explanation: YED > 1 indicates a luxury (income-elastic) good.


Question 555. A negative income elasticity of demand indicates:

A. A luxury good
B. An inferior good
C. A necessity
D. A complement

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Demand falls as income rises.


Question 556. A YED between 0 and 1 indicates:

A. A normal necessity
B. An inferior good
C. A luxury
D. A Giffen good

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Demand rises less than proportionally with income.


Question 557. Cross elasticity of demand measures:

A. Responsiveness of demand for one good to a price change of another
B. Responsiveness of supply
C. Responsiveness to income
D. Responsiveness to own price

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: XED = %ΔQx / %ΔPy.


Question 558. A positive cross elasticity of demand between two goods indicates they are:

A. Substitutes
B. Complements
C. Inferior
D. Unrelated

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: A rise in price of Y raises demand for X.


Question 559. A negative cross elasticity indicates goods are:

A. Unrelated
B. Luxuries
C. Substitutes
D. Complements

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: A rise in price of Y reduces demand for X.

B. -0.5, complements
C. 0.5, substitutes

Question 561. Price of cars rises 10%; demand for tyres falls 5%. XED is:

A. 2
B. 0.5
C. -0.5
D. -2

Question 562. A cross elasticity near zero suggests:

A. Strong substitutes
B. Strong complements
C. Luxuries
D. The goods are unrelated

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Price of one hardly affects demand for the other.


Question 563. Individual demands at P = N50: A = 3, B = 5, C = 2. At P = N40: A = 4, B = 7, C = 3. Market demand rises from:

B. 50 to 40
C. 3 to 4

Question 565. "Ostentatious" or snob goods relate to which exception to the law of demand?

B. Ignorance
C. Veblen effect

Question 566. Consumers who judge quality by price may buy more at a higher price. This is the exception of:

Question 601. Supply is best defined as:

B. Both Low

D. One High, one Low

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Low is a dominant strategy for each, though both High is better jointly.


Question 977. A dominant strategy is:

A. A random choice
B. The highest price
C. A collusive strategy
D. A strategy that gives the best payoff whatever rivals do

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: In the prisoners' dilemma, confessing/cheating dominates.


Question 978. The Cournot model of oligopoly assumes firms compete by choosing:

A. Locations
B. Prices
C. Advertising
D. Quantities

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Antoine Augustin Cournot (1838).


Question 979. The Bertrand model assumes firms compete by choosing:

A. Capacity only
B. Advertising
C. Prices
D. Quantities

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: With identical products, Bertrand competition can drive price to MC.


Question 980. Concentration ratio measures:

A. Number of consumers
B. The market share of the largest firms in an industry
C. Profit rate
D. Price elasticity

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: E.g. CR4 = combined share of the four largest firms.


Question 981. Four largest firms hold 30%, 25%, 15% and 10% of sales. The four-firm concentration ratio is:

A. 30%
B. 80%
C. 70%
D. 100%

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: 30 + 25 + 15 + 10 = 80%: highly concentrated (oligopoly).


Question 982. Non-price competition is especially common in oligopoly because:

A. Price wars are risky and rivals quickly match price cuts
B. Prices are set by law
C. Products are homogeneous
D. There is free entry

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Advertising and branding are safer.


Question 983. Which market structure has many firms, homogeneous products and free entry?

A. Oligopoly
B. Perfect competition
C. Monopolistic competition
D. Monopoly

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Monopolistic competition has differentiated products.


Question 984. Which market structure has many firms, differentiated products and free entry?

A. Monopolistic competition
B. Perfect competition
C. Oligopoly
D. Monopoly

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: E.g. restaurants.


Question 985. Which market structure has few firms, high barriers and interdependence?

A. Monopolistic competition
B. Perfect competition
C. Monopoly
D. Oligopoly

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Strategic behaviour is central.


Question 986. Which market structure has one firm and very high barriers?

A. Monopolistic competition
B. Oligopoly
C. Perfect competition
D. Monopoly

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: The firm is the industry.


Question 987. In which structure is the firm's demand curve the same as the market demand curve?

A. Monopoly
B. Perfect competition
C. Oligopoly
D. Monopolistic competition

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: The single firm faces all market demand.


Question 988. Advertising is least likely in:

A. Differentiated monopoly
B. Monopolistic competition
C. Oligopoly
D. Perfect competition

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Homogeneous products and perfect information make advertising pointless.


Question 989. In which market structure can firms earn supernormal profits in the long run?

A. Perfect competition
B. All structures equally
C. Monopolistic competition
D. Monopoly and (often) oligopoly

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Barriers to entry protect profits.


Question 990. Which structures earn only normal profit in the long run?

A. Perfect competition and monopolistic competition
B. Only oligopoly
C. Monopoly and oligopoly
D. Only monopoly

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Free entry eliminates supernormal profit.


Question 991. A market for mobile phone network services in Nigeria is best described as:

A. Monopolistic competition
B. Oligopoly
C. Pure monopoly
D. Perfect competition

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: A few large interdependent firms.


Question 992. A local market with many tailors offering slightly different styles is best described as:

A. Monopolistic competition
B. Monopoly
C. Perfect competition
D. Oligopoly

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Many sellers, differentiated products, easy entry.


Question 993. The national electricity transmission grid operated by one company is best described as:

A. Oligopoly
B. Perfect competition
C. Natural monopoly
D. Monopolistic competition

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Huge fixed costs favour a single supplier.


Question 994. Contestable markets theory (Baumol, 1982) argues that:

A. Concentration ratios determine behaviour completely
B. Monopoly is always harmful
C. Threat of entry can discipline firms even with few incumbents, if entry and exit are costless
D. Barriers do not matter at all

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Low sunk costs make markets contestable.


Question 995. Allocative efficiency (P = MC) holds in long-run equilibrium only under:

A. Oligopoly
B. Monopoly
C. Perfect competition
D. Monopolistic competition

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Other structures have P > MC.


Question 996. Consumer welfare is generally highest under:

A. Perfect competition, due to lowest price and highest output
B. Oligopoly with collusion
C. Monopoly
D. Cartel

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Monopolies may innovate, but static welfare is highest under competition.


Question 997. Competition law (antitrust) mainly aims to:

A. Create monopolies
B. Fix all prices
C. Prevent abuse of market power and collusion
D. Ban advertising

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: In Nigeria, the FCCPC enforces the 2018 Competition Act.


Question 998. (Revision) A cement industry has three firms with 85% market share. Prices change rarely, and firms advertise heavily. Which structure and model best fits?

A. Perfect competition; price-taking
B. Monopoly; price discrimination
C. Monopolistic competition; tangency solution
D. Oligopoly; kinked demand and non-price competition

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: High concentration and price rigidity indicate oligopoly.


Question 999. (Revision) Ranking structures from most to least competitive gives:

A. Oligopoly, perfect competition, monopoly, monopolistic competition
B. Perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, monopoly
C. Monopoly, oligopoly, monopolistic competition, perfect competition
D. Monopolistic competition, perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Number of firms falls and market power rises along this ranking.


Question 1000. A monopolistically competitive firm has MR = MC at 200 units, where price is N900 and ATC is N750. In the short run it earns:

A. Supernormal profit of N150
B. A loss of N30,000
C. Supernormal profit of N30,000
D. Normal profit only

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Profit = (900 - 750) x 200 = 30,000; entry will erode it in the long run.
