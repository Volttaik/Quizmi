Questions 001-100

Question 001. The study of African societies and cultures is best approached across which three broad historical settings?

A. Prehistoric, industrial and digital
B. Tribal, imperial and global only
C. Ancient, medieval and modern European
D. Traditional, colonial and contemporary

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: The course outline surveys African societies in traditional, colonial and contemporary settings.

Question 002. The "traditional" setting in African studies refers mainly to:

A. The period before significant European colonial influence
B. The period of the Atlantic slave trade only
C. Only the 21st century
D. The period after independence

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Traditional means pre-colonial ways of life rooted in indigenous institutions.

Question 003. Africa is the world's second-largest continent by both area and:

A. Population
B. Number of oceans
C. Industrial output
D. Number of deserts

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Africa ranks second to Asia in area and population.

Question 004. Africa currently has how many internationally recognised sovereign states (AU members)?

A. 40
B. 72
C. 55
D. 25

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: The African Union has 55 member states.

Question 005. Which conference of 1884–1885 formalised the European partition of Africa?

A. Bandung Conference
B. Yalta Conference
C. Paris Peace Conference
D. Berlin Conference

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Convened by Otto von Bismarck, it set rules for claiming African territory.

Question 006. The "Scramble for Africa" refers to:

A. The independence movements of the 1960s
B. Inter-ethnic migration within Africa
C. The rapid colonisation of Africa by European powers in the late 19th century
D. The spread of Islam across the Sahara

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Between about 1881 and 1914 most of Africa came under European control.

Question 007. Which two African countries were never formally colonised for a long period?

A. Senegal and Mali
B. Nigeria and Ghana
C. Ethiopia and Liberia
D. Kenya and Egypt

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Ethiopia defeated Italy at Adwa (1896); Liberia was founded by freed American slaves.

Question 008. The Battle of Adwa (1896) is significant because:

A. Ethiopia defeated an invading Italian army
B. The Zulu defeated the Boers
C. Britain conquered Benin
D. France annexed Algeria

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: It preserved Ethiopian independence and became a symbol of African resistance.

Question 009. "Indirect rule" was a colonial policy most associated with:

A. France in Senegal
B. Britain, especially under Lord Lugard in Northern Nigeria
C. Portugal in Angola
D. Belgium in Congo

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Britain governed through existing traditional rulers.

Question 010. Indirect rule worked best in Northern Nigeria because:

A. It was a French colony
B. It had no rulers at all
C. It already had centralised emirate administration
D. The people were Christians

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: The emirates had established hierarchies, taxation and courts.

Question 011. Indirect rule largely failed in Igboland because:

A. The Igbo were nomadic
B. Igbo society was largely decentralised without paramount chiefs
C. The Igbo had powerful kings everywhere
D. The British did not reach the East

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: The British created "warrant chiefs" who lacked traditional legitimacy.

Question 012. "Warrant chiefs" were:

A. Army generals
B. Elected parliamentarians
C. Colonial appointees given authority in societies without traditional kings
D. Priests of traditional shrines

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Their lack of legitimacy contributed to unrest like the 1929 Aba Women's Riot.

Question 013. The Aba Women's War of 1929 was mainly a protest against:

A. Fears of taxation of women and abuses by warrant chiefs
B. Independence leaders
C. Christian missionaries' schools
D. Railway construction

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: It showed women's organised political power in Igbo society.

Question 014. France's colonial policy aimed at making Africans culturally French was called:

A. Ujamaa
B. Apartheid
C. Indirect rule
D. Assimilation

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Assimilation sought to turn colonised peoples into French citizens culturally.

Question 015. The policy that later modified French assimilation, allowing some local distinctions, was:

A. Ubuntu
B. Partition
C. Negritude
D. Association

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Association retained some indigenous institutions under French control.

Question 016. Apartheid was a system of institutionalised racial segregation in:

A. South Africa
B. Nigeria
C. Ghana
D. Kenya

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Formally implemented from 1948 until the early 1990s.

Question 017. A key cultural effect of colonialism in Africa was:

A. Introduction of Western education, Christianity and European languages
B. Complete disappearance of African languages
C. End of all traditional religion
D. Return to hunting and gathering

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Colonialism introduced new institutions that coexisted with indigenous ones.

Question 018. Christian missionaries influenced African societies mainly through:

A. Military conquest only
B. Building pyramids
C. Banning all trade
D. Schools, hospitals and churches

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Mission education produced the early Western-educated African elite.

Question 019. Islam spread into West Africa mainly through:

A. Atlantic slave ships
B. British colonial schools
C. Trans-Saharan trade and Muslim scholars
D. Portuguese sailors

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Berber and Arab traders and clerics brought Islam southward from about the 8th century.

Question 020. The 1804 jihad that established the Sokoto Caliphate was led by:

A. Shaka Zulu
B. Usman dan Fodio
C. Mansa Musa
D. Samori Toure

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: It united much of Hausaland under a Fulani-led caliphate.

Question 021. The ancient empire famous for Mansa Musa's lavish pilgrimage to Mecca (1324) was:

A. Benin
B. Kanem-Bornu
C. Mali
D. Oyo

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: His gold distribution reportedly affected prices in Cairo.

Question 022. Which ancient West African empire was known as the "Land of Gold"?

A. Ghana
B. Zulu
C. Aksum
D. Buganda

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Old Ghana (Wagadu) controlled gold trade routes.

Question 023. Great Zimbabwe is evidence of:

A. A sophisticated pre-colonial state with monumental stone architecture
B. European construction in Africa
C. A modern city
D. A colonial fort

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: It flourished around the 11th–15th centuries.

Question 024. The Benin Kingdom is famous for its:

A. Bronze (brass) castings and plaques
B. Pyramids
C. Desert caravans
D. Rock-cut churches

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Many Benin Bronzes were looted by the British in 1897.

Question 025. The British punitive expedition of 1897 resulted in:

A. The abolition of slavery
B. The sacking of Benin City and looting of its artworks
C. The founding of Lagos
D. Independence of Nigeria

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Oba Ovonramwen was exiled to Calabar.

Question 026. The Nok culture of central Nigeria is known for:

A. Rock paintings only
B. Terracotta sculptures and early iron working
C. Bronze heads of Ife
D. Stone churches

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Nok dates from about 1500 BC to AD 500.

Question 027. The Ife bronze and terracotta heads are noted for their:

A. Use of plastic
B. Abstract geometric shapes only
C. European style
D. Naturalistic realism

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: They date from roughly the 12th–15th centuries.

Question 028. Igbo-Ukwu is an archaeological site known for:

A. Colonial forts
B. Intricate bronze objects dated to about the 9th–10th century
C. Stone pyramids
D. Oil wells

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: They are among the oldest bronze castings in West Africa.

Question 029. The "contemporary" setting of African societies is characterised by:

A. A blend of indigenous, Islamic and Western influences alongside urbanisation and globalisation
B. Complete return to pre-colonial life
C. Absence of states
D. Total Westernisation with no tradition

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Modern Africans often combine traditional and modern practices.

Question 030. Urbanisation affects African traditional culture mainly by:

A. Weakening extended family ties and communal controls
B. Strengthening village gerontocracy everywhere
C. Ending all migration
D. Abolishing languages overnight

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Migrants to cities adopt more individualised lifestyles.

Question 031. Ghana (Gold Coast) became the first sub-Saharan African colony to gain independence in:

A. 1960
B. 1957
C. 1945
D. 1975

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Led by Kwame Nkrumah.

Question 032. 1960 is often called the "Year of Africa" because:

A. Seventeen African countries gained independence that year
B. The Berlin Conference took place
C. The AU was founded
D. Apartheid ended

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Nigeria was among them, on 1 October 1960.

Question 033. Pan-Africanism is the idea that:

A. Africa should reject all cultures
B. Only Arabs should rule Africa
C. Africa should be divided among Europeans
D. People of African descent share common interests and should unite

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Promoted by figures like W.E.B. Du Bois, Marcus Garvey and Nkrumah.

Question 034. Negritude was a cultural movement that:

A. Supported colonial rule
B. Promoted French assimilation
C. Celebrated Black African identity and values
D. Rejected African art

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Founded by Léopold Senghor, Aimé Césaire and Léon Damas.

Question 035. "African personality" as a concept was popularised by:

A. Cecil Rhodes
B. Lord Lugard
C. Kwame Nkrumah (and earlier Edward Blyden)
D. Napoleon

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: It asserted distinct African values and dignity.

Question 036. Ethnocentric colonial writers often described African societies as "primitive". Modern scholars reject this because:

A. All cultures are identical
B. African societies had complex political, economic and religious systems
C. Africa had no culture
D. Colonial writers were always accurate

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Judging cultures by European standards is ethnocentric.

Question 037. The Sahara Desert divides Africa broadly into:

A. North Africa and Sub-Saharan Africa
B. Islands and mainland
C. Asia and Africa
D. East and West Europe

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: North Africa is largely Arab-Berber; Sub-Saharan Africa is culturally diverse.

Question 038. North African societies are culturally influenced mostly by:

A. Khoisan culture
B. Arab-Islamic civilisation
C. Portuguese culture only
D. Bantu migrations

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Arab conquests from the 7th century spread Islam and Arabic.

Question 039. The Bantu migration refers to:

A. Movement of Arabs to Spain
B. Migration of Europeans to Africa
C. Slave trade to America
D. The spread of Bantu-speaking peoples from West-Central Africa to much of central, eastern and southern Africa

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: It spread farming, iron working and Bantu languages over centuries.

Question 040. The original homeland of Bantu-speaking peoples is thought to be around:

A. Egypt
B. Morocco
C. Madagascar
D. The Nigeria–Cameroon border region

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Linguistic evidence points to the Benue–Cross River area.

Question 041. The Atlantic slave trade affected African societies by:

A. Ending all wars
B. Creating industrial growth in Africa
C. Depopulating regions, encouraging warfare and disrupting economies
D. Increasing population in West Africa

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Millions were forcibly taken to the Americas.

Question 042. Which commodity was central to the "legitimate trade" that replaced the slave trade in the 19th century?

A. Palm oil
B. Crude oil
C. Coffee from Brazil
D. Cotton cloth from India

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Palm oil from the Niger Delta lubricated British industry.

Question 043. The "Oil Rivers" in 19th-century Nigerian history referred to:

A. Rivers in Northern Nigeria
B. The Nile Delta
C. The Niger Delta area famous for palm oil trade
D. Crude oil fields of Kano

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: The name came from palm oil, not petroleum.

Question 044. Jaja of Opobo is remembered as:

A. A Hausa emir
B. A powerful Niger Delta trader-king who resisted British control
C. A Yoruba priest
D. A Zulu general

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: He was deported by the British in 1887.

Question 045. Cultural continuity in contemporary Africa is shown when:

A. Indigenous languages are banned
B. People hold traditional naming ceremonies alongside church or mosque rites
C. People reject family ties
D. All festivals are abandoned

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Old practices persist in adapted forms.

Question 046. Cultural change occurs through all of the following processes EXCEPT:

A. Innovation
B. Diffusion
C. Cultural stagnation
D. Acculturation

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Stagnation is the absence of change.

Question 047. Acculturation refers to:

A. Destroying all culture
B. Inventing new tools
C. Cultural change resulting from continuous contact between cultures
D. Learning one's own culture from birth

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Colonial contact caused extensive acculturation in Africa.

Question 048. Enculturation refers to:

A. Forced conversion
B. The process by which individuals learn their own culture
C. Borrowing from foreign cultures
D. Urban migration

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: It happens through family, peers and community.

Question 049. Diffusion is:

A. A type of marriage
B. The spread of cultural elements from one society to another
C. Inheritance of land
D. Disappearance of culture

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: E.g. the spread of maize and cassava from the Americas into Africa.

Question 050. Cassava and maize became important African food crops after being introduced from:

A. Europe's own farms
B. Australia
C. China
D. The Americas, via the Portuguese

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: This is an example of cultural diffusion.

Question 051. The Swahili culture of East Africa developed from interaction between:

A. Egyptians and Romans only
B. Yoruba and Hausa
C. Europeans and Khoisan
D. Bantu Africans and Arab/Persian traders

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Swahili language has a Bantu base with many Arabic loanwords.

Question 052. Timbuktu became famous historically as:

A. A port on the Atlantic
B. A colonial capital
C. A Christian monastery
D. A centre of Islamic scholarship and trade

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Sankore University and many manuscripts flourished there.

Question 053. The Kingdom of Aksum in present-day Ethiopia/Eritrea is notable for:

A. Early adoption of Christianity and giant stone stelae
B. Bronze heads
C. Being a French colony
D. Trans-Saharan gold

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Aksum adopted Christianity in the 4th century.

Question 054. The Zulu kingdom rose to power in southern Africa under:

A. Sundiata
B. Shaka
C. Mansa Musa
D. Menelik II

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Shaka reorganised the military in the early 19th century.

Question 055. The Mfecane refers to:

A. A colonial law
B. A period of widespread upheaval and migration in southern Africa in the early 19th century
C. A West African festival
D. A type of marriage

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Associated with the rise of the Zulu state.

Question 056. The Oyo Empire's military strength rested significantly on its:

A. Air force
B. Cavalry
C. Camels
D. Navy

Explanation: Horses were obtained through trade with the north.

Question 057. The Oyo Mesi in the Oyo Empire was:

A. A council of chiefs that checked the Alaafin's power
B. The army commander

Question 058. The Kanem-Bornu Empire was located around:

A. The Niger Delta
B. Lake Chad
C. The Cape of Good Hope
D. The Nile Delta

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: It lasted from roughly the 9th to the 19th century.

Question 059. Colonialism created the modern boundaries of most African states, which often:

A. Were chosen by Africans
B. Matched ethnic boundaries perfectly
C. Followed natural cultural regions exactly
D. Split ethnic groups across different countries

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: E.g. the Yoruba are found in Nigeria and Benin; the Hausa in Nigeria and Niger.

Question 060. The Somali, Maasai and Ewe are examples of peoples who:

A. Live only on islands
B. Have no culture
C. Were never colonised
D. Were split by colonial borders across more than one country

Explanation: Arbitrary borders divided them.

Question 061. The 1914 amalgamation of Nigeria was carried out by:

A. Cecil Rhodes
B. Richard Lander
C. Mungo Park
D. Frederick Lugard

Explanation: Northern and Southern Protectorates were merged.

Question 062. The name "Nigeria" was suggested by:

A. Lord Lugard himself
B. Flora Shaw
C. Queen Victoria
D. Herbert Macaulay

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Flora Shaw, later Lugard's wife, coined it in 1897.

Question 063. Western education in colonial Nigeria spread faster in the South than the North partly because:

A. The South had no schools
B. Northern emirs restricted Christian missionary activity

Explanation: Lugard's agreement limited missions in Muslim areas.

Question 064. Contemporary African societies face the challenge of "cultural erosion", meaning:

A. Strengthening of elders' authority
B. Loss of indigenous languages, values and practices
C. Increased oral literature
D. Growth of traditional festivals

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Globalisation and media accelerate this.

Question 065. Globalisation affects African culture by:

A. Stopping technological change
B. Ending all cultural exchange
C. Spreading foreign music, dress, food and values through media and trade
D. Isolating Africa completely

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: It can both erode and promote African culture globally.

Question 066. Nollywood is an example of contemporary African culture:

A. Spreading African stories and values globally through film
B. Being a colonial invention
C. Destroying all African identity
D. Banning local languages

Explanation: It is one of the world's largest film industries by volume.

Question 067. Afrobeats music's global popularity shows that:

A. Only Western music matters

D. Culture never travels

Question 068. The term "cultural revival" in Africa refers to:

A. Military rule

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: E.g. FESTAC '77 in Lagos.

Question 069. FESTAC '77 was:

B. A colonial exhibition

Question 074. Ali Mazrui was a scholar from:

Question 101. Social organisation refers to:

A. The physical layout of a village only
B. The patterned ways in which relationships among individuals and groups are structured in a society

D. A government ministry

Explanation: It includes kinship, age grades, associations and status systems.

Question 102. The most basic unit of social organisation in traditional African societies is:

A. The family
B. The army
C. The state
D. The market

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Families combine into lineages, clans and communities.

Question 975. Which statement about African women in traditional society is most accurate?

A. They had no roles
B. They ruled all societies
C. They had significant economic and some political roles, though within patriarchal structures
D. They were all secluded

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: E.g. Iyalode, market women, Umuada.

Question 976. Witchcraft beliefs, polygyny and food taboos are best understood by applying:

A. Ignoring context
B. Cultural relativism while also considering human rights
C. Ethnocentrism
D. Biological explanations only

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Understand practices in context, evaluate harm.

Question 977. The Bantu migration, the trans-Saharan trade and the Atlantic trade together explain:

A. Only European history
B. Major historical processes shaping African peoples and cultures
C. Recent elections
D. Modern industry

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: They spread people, goods, religions and ideas.

Question 978. Which best explains why Hausa became a lingua franca in Northern Nigeria?

A. Igbo traders spread it
B. Trade networks, Islamic scholarship and political dominance of Hausa states
C. It is the oldest language in Africa
D. Colonial schools taught only Hausa

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Commerce and religion spread languages.

Question 979. In comparing the Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo, which is correct?

A. Igbo are mainly Muslim
B. All three are mainly traditionalist
C. All three are mainly Muslim
D. Hausa are mainly Muslim; Yoruba are mixed Muslim and Christian; Igbo are mainly Christian

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Religious composition differs.

Question 980. A practice where a widow is expected to marry her late husband's brother would most likely be found in a society that is:

A. Industrialised and secular
B. Without marriage
C. Patrilineal with bridewealth linking the wife to the husband's lineage
D. Matrilineal with no bridewealth

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Levirate supports patrilineal continuity.

Question 981. Which pair correctly matches concept and example?

A. Folkways – incest taboo
B. Rite of passage – naming ceremony
C. Cultural lag – Bayajidda legend
D. Totemism – Ifa divination

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Naming ceremony marks incorporation of a newborn.

Question 982. The presence of Yoruba-derived religions in Brazil and Cuba demonstrates:

A. The absence of African influence abroad
B. The global spread of African culture through the diaspora
C. Colonial invention
D. That Yoruba culture is foreign

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Orisha traditions survived slavery.

Question 983. The Nigerian constitutional principle of federal character responds to which feature of Nigerian society?

A. Religious uniformity
B. Ethnic and regional diversity
C. Lack of languages
D. A single ethnic group

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: It aims at fair representation.

Question 984. Which feature is common to most traditional African societies studied in this course?

A. Written legal codes everywhere
B. Strong kinship ties and communal values
C. Absence of religion
D. Industrial economies

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: Kinship is foundational.

Question 985. Which is the best evidence that culture is dynamic in Africa?

A. Cultures never changing
B. Refusal to adopt any new tools
C. Traditional attire like aso-oke being reinvented in modern fashion
D. Disappearance of all festivals

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Old elements take new forms.

Question 986. An anthropologist studying a Tiv village by living there for a year uses:

A. Library research only
B. Laboratory experiment
C. Telephone survey
D. Participant observation

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: A classic ethnographic method.

Question 987. Why should students avoid calling Nigerian ethnic groups "tribes" in academic writing?

A. The term carries colonial, pejorative connotations
B. It is too specific
C. It refers to nations only
D. It is a modern term

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: "Ethnic group" is preferred.

Question 988. The Ekpe society of the Efik shows that secret societies could perform:

A. No functions
B. Only farming
C. Political, economic and judicial functions
D. Only entertainment

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Ekpe enforced debt payments in trade.

Question 989. A Kanuri person from Borno and an Edo person from Benin share:

A. Identical festivals
B. The same traditional ruler
C. Nigerian citizenship but different languages and historical traditions
D. The same language

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Nigeria unites diverse peoples.

Question 990. The Sokoto Caliphate's establishment in 1804–1812 transformed Hausaland mainly by:

A. Uniting Hausa states under Islamic Fulani-led rule
B. Creating acephalous villages
C. Introducing Christianity
D. Abolishing Islam

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: The caliphate shaped northern Nigeria's culture.

Question 991. Which approach best describes how this course views African societies?

A. Only through modern statistics
B. Only as primitive
C. Holistically, examining social, political, economic and religious life across time
D. Only through colonial records

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: The course outline integrates many dimensions.

Question 992. The practice of age grades among the Igbo and Maasai shows that:

A. Similar institutions may develop in different African societies
B. Age has no meaning
C. Age grades were introduced by Europeans
D. All Africans are Igbo

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Comparative study reveals parallels.

Question 993. Which best explains why traditional healers remain popular in contemporary Nigeria?

A. Cultural beliefs about illness, accessibility and cost
B. Hospitals do not exist anywhere
C. Healers are required by law
D. Medicine was banned

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Medical pluralism persists.

Question 994. If a Yoruba family will not eat a particular animal because of a lineage history, this is an example of:

A. A fashion choice
B. A market rule
C. A colonial law
D. A lineage food taboo (eewo)

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Taboos maintain identity and spiritual relations.

Question 995. The Oyo Mesi and the Ogboni demonstrate that Yoruba kingship was:

A. Non-existent
B. Controlled by Europeans
C. Constitutional, with checks on the ruler's power
D. Absolute and unchecked

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Rulers could be deposed.

Question 996. Which statement correctly contrasts traditional and contemporary African economies?

A. Both are identical
B. Contemporary economies use only barter
C. Traditional economies were largely subsistence-based; contemporary ones are monetised and integrated into global markets
D. Traditional economies were industrial

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Colonialism drove monetisation.

Question 997. A student who wants to "demonstrate total understanding of the peoples of Nigeria" should be able to:

A. Name only three ethnic groups
B. Recite colonial dates only
C. Ignore traditional religion
D. Describe their origins, social structures, beliefs, occupations and changes over time

Correct Answer: D

Explanation: Integrated knowledge is required.

Question 998. Which value is captured by the Igbo name "Onyekachi" (who is greater than God) ?

A. The supremacy of God in Igbo belief
B. Wealth above God
C. Market days
D. Polygyny

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Names express theology.

Question 999. The idea that Africa is "one country with one culture" is wrong because:

A. Africa has 55 states and thousands of ethnic groups with varied cultures
B. Africa is in Asia
C. Africa has no states
D. Africa has only one language

Correct Answer: A

Explanation: Diversity is fundamental.

Question 1000. Overall, studying African societies and cultures helps students to:

A. Reject their culture
B. Ignore history
C. Appreciate diversity, understand their heritage and promote peaceful coexistence
D. Despise other groups

Correct Answer: C

Explanation: Cultural understanding builds unity.
